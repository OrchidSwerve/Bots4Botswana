Our Story

Built by a student who wanted more kids to have what got them started.

Bots4Botswana began with a simple observation: robotics kits that changed one student's path were sitting unused in closets across the country, while students elsewhere had none at all.

Where it started

Robotics was the class that made engineering click. Building a robot that could actually navigate a course, debugging code until a sensor finally behaved, working through failed builds with a team — it turned abstract math and science into something concrete and, honestly, fun. That experience shaped a path toward STEM that might not have happened without access to a kit and a team to build with.

Years of competing on robotics teams meant seeing, up close, how much of the learning happens through the equipment itself — not just instruction, but hands-on iteration. It also meant seeing how many retired kits end up in storage once a team upgrades or a program ends, still fully usable but no longer needed.

Why Botswana

Through robotics community connections, it became clear that schools in Botswana had real enthusiasm for STEM programs but limited access to the physical kits needed to run them. That gap — equipment sitting idle in one place, demand for it in another — was the entire premise for Bots4Botswana: connect the two directly, rather than starting a robotics supply chain from zero.

Building it, one partnership at a time

What began as reaching out to a few robotics teams and asking if they had old kits to spare grew into a small, organized collection effort — coordinating pickups, learning how to test and refurbish EV3 and NXT hardware, and figuring out the logistics of getting equipment internationally. Along the way, partnerships with robotics organizations like Swerve Robotics made it possible to collect kits at a scale one student couldn't have managed alone.

The next step was making sure a kit's arrival wasn't the end of the story — building tutorials and teacher guides so that a school receiving its first robotics kit would have everything needed to put it to use right away, not just a box of parts.

What this is

A student-led initiative: not a school project and not a large organization, but something in between: run with the seriousness of a real nonprofit, and the flexibility of a small, hands-on team.

What guides it

Every decision — which kits to collect, how to allocate donations, which schools to partner with — is made with one question in mind: does this get more students building and learning?

Real-world impact, kept honest

We'd rather report our real numbers — 60+ kits collected, 11 expansion kits, a handful of partner schools — than round up or imply a scale we haven't reached yet. See exactly where things stand on the Impact page.

Student-Led, Seriously Run

This is a student initiative — but it's run with the planning, transparency, and follow-through of an organization built to last.
