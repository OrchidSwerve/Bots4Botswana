Bringing Robotics Education to Students in Botswana

Bots4Botswana is a student-led initiative expanding access to robotics education — collecting, refurbishing, and donating LEGO Mindstorms EV3 and NXT kits, alongside free learning resources, to underserved students in Botswana.

Why It Matters

Robotics kits don't just teach code — they teach how to think.

Robotics education gives students hands-on experience with engineering, programming, and problem-solving — skills that open doors to further study and careers in STEM. For many students, the barrier isn't ability, it's access to the equipment needed to learn by building.

We chose to focus on Botswana because of a direct connection to local robotics teams and educators who identified real, specific need: schools with the enthusiasm to run robotics programs but no kits to run them with. Rather than starting from scratch, we're putting equipment already sitting unused in the U.S. into the hands of students ready to use it.

A single donated kit doesn't stop at one lesson. Once refurbished and paired with our tutorials, it supports an entire classroom, semester after semester, as students rotate through building and programming challenges.

Step 01

A kit is collected

Retired or unused LEGO Mindstorms EV3 and NXT kits are donated by robotics teams, schools, and individuals across the U.S.

Step 02

It's refurbished & tested

Every kit is inspected, cleaned, and quality-checked piece by piece before it's cleared for shipment.

Step 03

It reaches a classroom

Kits are shipped to partner schools in Botswana along with tutorials and teacher guides.

The Route

From collection points in the U.S. to classrooms in Botswana

Every kit follows the same path — donated locally, refurbished by our team, then shipped over 14,000 kilometers to reach the students who'll use it.

Featured Impact

One kit, a full classroom of engineers

A single donated EV3 kit gives a class the chance to work through real engineering challenges: building a chassis that can navigate a course, writing the code that tells it where to go, and debugging why a sensor isn't reading correctly. Those are the same fundamentals used in university engineering programs and robotics careers — introduced years earlier than most students would otherwise encounter them.

Because the kits are reusable, that same equipment supports new students and new challenges every term, and our tutorials mean a teacher doesn't need a robotics background to run a great lesson.
