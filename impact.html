Impact

What happens after a kit is donated

Every robotics kit we collect follows the same path to a student's hands — and the learning doesn't stop once it arrives. Here's how a single donation becomes years of classroom use.

From donated kit to long-term learning

Each stage exists to make sure the kit that arrives is complete, working, and immediately usable in a classroom.

01

Donated Kits

Robotics teams, schools, and individuals donate retired EV3 and NXT kits.

02

Refurbishment

Kits are cleaned, inspected, and missing pieces replaced.

03

Shipping

Complete kits are packed and shipped to partner schools in Botswana.

04

Schools

Kits arrive alongside tutorials and teacher guides.

05

Students

Students build, program, and troubleshoot real robots.

06

Long-Term STEM Learning

The kit stays in rotation, supporting new students term after term.

Who receives the kits

Partner schools in Botswana identified through robotics-community connections — schools with teachers and students ready to run a program, but without the equipment to start one.

How students use them

Kits are used for structured build-and-program challenges: designing a chassis, wiring sensors, writing logic to complete a course, and debugging when something doesn't work as expected.

What skills they learn

Engineering design, programming fundamentals, systematic debugging, and the collaborative problem-solving that comes from working through a build as a team.
